Evidence boundary: Likerts creates synthetic, directional output. Simulation units are not surveyed respondents, web sources are not independently verified, and the result is not a representative market estimate.

Use Likerts when you need a fast first read on a positioning idea, feature concept, adoption barrier, or audience tension—and you want to see the assumptions and source trail behind that read.

A research workflow, not a magic audience.

Each run keeps retrieval, assumptions, model inference, and review distinct so the output is easier to challenge and use responsibly.

1

Market-aware source context

Two bounded searches seek a neutral evidence view plus market context or disconfirming evidence. URLs and excerpts remain visible in the ledger.

2

Synthetic concept test

A model creates a five-point directional distribution, exploratory segments, illustrative reasoning, and explicit cautions for the chosen audience.

3

Separate model review

A distinct review call checks overclaiming, unsupported grounding, stereotypes, arithmetic, and contradictions before the result is labelled complete.

Fast enough for exploration. Bounded enough for serious teams.

Likerts is appropriate for early discovery and research planning. It is deliberately explicit about where synthetic research stops.

Useful for

Concept screening, message exploration, interview-guide preparation, adoption-barrier hypotheses, alternative explanations, and prioritising what to validate next.

Not evidence for

Population prevalence, statistical significance, customer quotes, causal claims, market sizing, or consequential decisions without appropriate human validation.

Questions teams should ask

01

Is this a human research panel?

No. Likerts does not recruit, survey, or observe people. Every distribution, segment, and verbatim is model-generated.

02

What does “grounded” mean here?

Current public text can inform the run and is disclosed in the ledger. It remains unverified context—not proof, endorsement, or a representative finding.

03

What should happen after a run?

Turn the most decision-relevant hypothesis into interviews, a properly sampled survey, behavioural analysis, usability work, or an experiment.
