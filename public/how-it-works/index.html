How it works

Start with a question worth testing.

Likerts is free to use and needs no account. It gives you a structured way to explore a directional audience hypothesis, then make a better plan to validate it with real people.

Try a studyRead the methodology

A useful output begins with a narrow decision, a defined audience, and a willingness to treat the result as a starting point—not a verdict.

The workflow

01

Write the decision question

Ask about a choice, behaviour, or perceived trade-off. Questions such as “What might make this audience hesitate?” lead to more useful hypotheses than asking for a market forecast.

02

Choose an audience and assumptions

Set the audience frame and list any premises you want considered. Treat simulation units as a control for the generated exercise, not as a real respondent count.

03

Add optional source context

Supply relevant public URLs if you have them. The workflow may retrieve public context when available and records the result in an evidence ledger. Context is not independent verification.

04

Inspect the directional output

Read the synthetic distribution, segments, themes, and cautions alongside their evidence classes. A methodological review stage checks for coherence and overclaiming.

05

Plan real validation

Use the output to decide whom to interview, what to survey, or which experiment to run. Important decisions should be checked with appropriate human research.

A strong brief is specific and falsifiable

More useful

“Among operations managers at small logistics firms, which onboarding concerns might reduce willingness to trial a shared workflow product?”

Less useful

“Will everyone love our product?” It has no defined audience, decision, timeframe, or counterargument to investigate.

Keep the output proportional to the input. A well-framed synthetic study can suggest what to test next; it cannot turn an uncertain premise into a validated fact.

Explore a hypothesis in a few minutes.

No account is required. Keep the evidence boundary visible, and take the next step with real-world research.
