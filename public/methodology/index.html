Methodology

Directional research, with the boundary in view.

Likerts helps frame and explore audience hypotheses. It keeps source context, user assumptions, model inference, and methodological review distinct so the output is easier to interpret responsibly.

Important: Likerts does not survey people or create a representative sample. Its distributions, segments, and verbatims are synthetic model output—not observed human responses or customer testimony.

The tool is designed for early exploration: clarifying a question, surfacing plausible reasons to investigate, and deciding what deserves follow-up with real participants. It is not a replacement for primary research.

How a study is produced

01

Frame the question

A research question and selected audience are shaped into a neutral study frame. The tool records the decision context and makes its assumptions explicit.

02

Separate context from claims

You may add public URLs. The workflow can also try to retrieve public context. Retrieved text and supplied URLs are treated as untrusted inputs—not as independently verified evidence or proof of a claim.

03

Generate a synthetic panel

A model creates a directional five-point Likert-style distribution, illustrative segments, explanations, and cautions. The simulation-unit count is a modeling control, not a count of surveyed people.

04

Apply methodological review

A separate review stage checks the candidate output for overclaiming, unsupported grounding, stereotypes, arithmetic issues, and contradictions. Review improves coherence; it does not validate the result against people or the web.

What the evidence ledger means

Every run classifies its inputs and output so a reader can see what is context, what is an assumption, and what is model inference.

Provided or retrieved source

Public text used for grounding. It can add context, but it is not independently verified by the study and does not establish a human finding.

User assumption

A premise supplied in the brief. It remains visible because assumptions may be useful, uncertain, or in need of validation.

Model inference

The synthetic distribution, segments, themes, and illustrative verbatims. These are generated hypotheses, not observed evidence.

What Likerts does not establish

It can help you

  • Turn a broad prompt into a researchable hypothesis.
  • Identify potential motivations, barriers, and segments to investigate.
  • Prepare a more specific plan for interviews, surveys, or experiments.

It cannot tell you

  • What a real population thinks or how many people hold a view.
  • Whether a finding is statistically significant, representative, causal, or current.
  • Whether a source is accurate, complete, or independently corroborated.

Use it to choose the next human-research step.

Start with a decision you need to make, inspect the boundaries in the result, then validate any consequential hypothesis with the people you serve.
