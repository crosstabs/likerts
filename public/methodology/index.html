Methodology

Directional research, with the boundary in view.

Likerts helps frame and explore audience hypotheses. It keeps source context, user assumptions, model inference, and methodological review distinct so the output is easier to interpret responsibly.

Important: Likerts does not survey people or create a representative sample. Its distributions, segments, and verbatims are synthetic model output—not observed human responses or customer testimony.

The tool is designed for early exploration: clarifying a question, surfacing plausible reasons to investigate, and deciding what deserves follow-up with real participants. It is not a replacement for primary research.

How a study is produced

01

Build the Population Frame

Before model calls, Likerts records the intended population, geography, languages, source datasets, available marginals and intersections, weighting status, coverage date, and unsupported characteristics. If structured population evidence is absent, fit remains explicitly unmeasured.

02

Select the research method

A runtime-owned template defines the required stimulus, estimand, scale or output structure, chart, critic criteria, limitations, and recommended human-validation design. Ranking, matrix, price-ladder, instrument-review, and interview-guide methods do not manufacture a generic Likert distribution.

03

Separate context from claims

You may add public URLs or bounded text, Markdown, CSV, JSON, PDF, DOCX, and XLSX research materials. Documents are extracted locally with page, paragraph, or cell locators; formulas, macros, scripts, links, and embedded objects are not executed. Raw uploaded files remain in the browser, and only retrieved bounded excerpts enter the run. All supplied or retrieved text remains untrusted grounding—not verified truth or human validation.

04

Generate method-specific model output

Directional methods may return a five-point distribution and modeled segment hypotheses. Other methods return ranked items, an anchored attribute matrix, a price ladder, an instrument review, or an interview guide. Quick and Deep change the bounded model work, never the evidence class: model-run cells are not surveyed people.

05

Measure disagreement and review

Deep mode reports distribution spread across model cells. A separate model stage then checks the candidate for overclaiming, unsupported grounding, stereotypes, arithmetic issues, and contradictions. These are internal diagnostics, not validation against people or the web.

06

Explore a modeled segment

After an eligible directional run, a selected model-constructed segment can answer bounded follow-ups, objections, explicit counterfactuals, or two-concept comparisons. Prior turns remain in context, and each answer shows the declared evidence and assumptions it used. Every answer is labeled “Model-generated perspective—not a participant quotation.” An optional local project can persist, export, import, or clear this context; browser storage is not encrypted account storage.

07

Prepare validation with real people

Every completed run creates a researcher-review draft containing a questionnaire, screening criteria, monitor-only demographic targets, transparent sample-planning assumptions, recruitment instructions, an analysis plan, provider links, and interoperable exports. Incidence remains unestimated and no participant panel is booked or connected.

What Population Fit means

Population Fit is a decomposed audit of declared demographic or firmographic coverage, source quality, intersections, and weighting convergence. It is not an accuracy score for the synthetic answers. Demographic fit does not prove attitudinal accuracy.

What the evidence ledger means

Every run classifies its inputs and output so a reader can see what is context, what is an assumption, and what is model inference.

Provided or retrieved source

Public text or a bounded excerpt from user-provided research material. It can add context, but it is not independently verified by the study and does not establish a human finding.

User assumption

A premise supplied in the brief. It remains visible because assumptions may be useful, uncertain, or in need of validation.

Model inference

The synthetic distribution, segments, themes, and illustrative verbatims. These are generated hypotheses, not observed evidence.

What Likerts does not establish

It can help you

  • Turn a broad prompt into a researchable hypothesis.
  • Identify potential motivations, barriers, and segments to investigate.
  • Prepare a more specific plan for interviews, surveys, or experiments.

It cannot tell you

  • What a real population thinks or how many people hold a view.
  • Whether a finding is statistically significant, representative, causal, or current.
  • Whether a source is accurate, complete, or independently corroborated.

Use it to choose the next human-research step.

Start with a decision you need to make, inspect the boundaries in the result, then validate any consequential hypothesis with the people you serve.
