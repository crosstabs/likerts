Open Likerts

Research standards

Open synthetic research standards, attached to every run.

Likerts makes the inputs, source trail, model routes, uncertainty, repeatability signals, and operating cost inspectable. The goal is a stronger hypothesis—not manufactured certainty.

Non-negotiable boundary: model-run cells are not human respondents. A synthetic distribution is not a population estimate, sample, survey result, or substitute for appropriate primary research. Demographic fit does not prove attitudinal accuracy.

A useful synthetic-research system should show how a result was produced, what it relied on, where its models disagreed, and what it cannot establish. These are the public rules Likerts is designed to follow.

Two modes, one evidence boundary.

ModeDesigned forModel workWhat it adds
Quick modeFast, low-cost hypothesis explorationBounded retrieval, framing, one aggregate model-synthesis generation, and reviewA directional first read with provenance and cautions
Deep modeQuestions where model disagreement is itself usefulMultiple independently generated model-run cells, aggregation, stability diagnostics, and reviewMore model diversity and an explicit view of spread—not human validation

Deep mode increases inference work and operator cost. It does not turn synthetic output into observed evidence or eliminate model bias.

The run receipt.

When the runtime supplies a field, the report and exported evidence pack retain it. Older or degraded runs may show a field as unavailable instead of inventing a value.

Input lineage
A canonical input hash, study and run identifiers, locale, market, and mode.
Population lineage
A versioned Population Frame and hash, declared source datasets, marginals and intersections, weighting diagnostics, unsupported characteristics, coverage date, and decomposed Population Fit.
Model Card
Runtime and prompt/schema versions, grounding mode, permitted and prohibited uses, and explicit attitudinal-validation status.
Evidence lineage
A stable evidence hash, disclosed URLs and excerpts, acquisition class, and retrieval events. Source text remains unverified context.
Model lineage
The requested route, fallbacks, resolved model where reported, stage status, and versioned prompt/schema identifiers.
Uncertainty
Confidence boundaries plus disagreement or stability measurements when multiple independent model cells ran.
Economics
Token use and the actual Vercel AI Gateway cost when provider metadata is returned. Otherwise cost is labelled estimated or unavailable.

Auditable replay, not deterministic output.

Stable inputs

Canonical hashes help show whether the brief and evidence packet changed between runs.

Variable inference

Language-model execution is not deterministic across time, providers, routes, or repeated calls—even with the same brief.

Measured spread

Where multiple cells or repeats exist, distribution distance and maximum spread describe agreement without turning it into accuracy.

Evaluation before claims.

Automated checks can test

  • Request and response contracts, safe failure, and five-point arithmetic.
  • Presence of provenance, evidence classes, model lineage, uncertainty, and cost metadata.
  • Language and script expectations across supported locales.
  • Repeatability distances across captured runs and unsupported human-panel wording.

They cannot establish

  • Agreement with a population that was not sampled.
  • Accuracy against an unnamed, mismatched, or proprietary panel.
  • Representativeness, statistical significance, causality, or source truth.
  • Fitness for consequential decisions without evidence appropriate to the stakes.

Official population data is registry-controlled.

Only reviewed, registry-owned records can be presented as curated official population constraints. The machine-readable population-data registry currently publishes an explicit zero state; user-supplied and retrieved sources cannot upgrade themselves to curated status.

Observed human-survey comparisons need a protocol.

A public benchmark is only meaningful when it records the dataset and licence, population and geography, field dates, exact wording and response scale, weighting, synthetic prompt, matching assumptions, and comparison metric. Likerts must report mismatches and null results—not just favourable examples. The machine-readable benchmark roadmap is the canonical record of candidate datasets and comparison status.

What completed comparisons will report

Distribution error
Mean absolute percentage-point error, maximum category error, total-variation distance, and Jensen-Shannon divergence.
Top-two-box error
Absolute percentage-point error only where question wording, ordered scale, and top-two mapping match exactly.
Rank agreement
Spearman agreement across at least three preregistered matched questions, with ties handled explicitly.
Segment direction
Agreement across preregistered, evaluable subgroup contrasts after applying a declared tie band.
Repeat variation
Descriptive spread across at least three runs with identical frozen lineage and evidence.
Evidence sensitivity
Movement across paired evidence variants while other recorded lineage stays fixed; this is not an accuracy metric.
Date and lineage
Evaluation date plus dataset, frame, runtime, model, prompt, schema, protocol, and scorer versions.

Inspect the full protocol, zero-state, access notes, and metric definitions in the machine-readable benchmark manifest.

Agent and API access.

Agents can validate a brief, run the bounded research workflow, or request a grounded model-segment follow-up through the remote Likerts MCP endpoint. The server endpoint is stateless. In the browser, people may optionally save bounded follow-up context in local IndexedDB projects with export, import, retention and deletion controls; this is local browser data, not encrypted account storage. Every turn remains a synthetic perspective—not an interview or participant quotation. Machine-readable product guidance is published in llms.txt and llms-full.txt. Anonymous access remains subject to rate, concurrency, and budget safeguards; availability is not an entitlement.
