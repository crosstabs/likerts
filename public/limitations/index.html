Evidence boundaries

Useful for exploration. Not evidence about people.

Likerts creates synthetic, directional output. It is intentionally not positioned as a survey, a sampled panel, a representative estimate, or a replacement for decisions that require human research.

Use the output as a hypothesis: validate material claims, rankings, messages, and decisions with appropriate real-world evidence.

The important limitations

1

No human respondents

Scores, segments, and verbatims are generated by a model. They are not survey answers, interview notes, customer quotes, or behavioural observations.

2

No sampling inference

There is no recruited population, sampling frame, response rate, margin of error, or statistical significance. Simulation units are not people.

3

No causal proof

A directional pattern does not show that a factor causes an outcome. It can point to an idea that deserves a controlled test or direct inquiry.

4

Source context is not verification

Supplied or retrieved public text may inform a run, but it is untrusted context. Likerts does not independently fact-check, corroborate, or certify it.

5

Models can be wrong or biased

Generated output can reflect omissions, framing effects, common assumptions, or model limitations. Review helps flag issues; it cannot eliminate them.

6

Outputs can vary

Replaying the same brief can produce different synthetic output. Treat any single run as exploratory, and preserve the assumptions and source ledger when comparing runs.

A safer way to use a result

A

Read the cautions and evidence classes first

Check whether a point comes from a source, an explicit assumption, or model inference. Do not turn a model inference into a statement about a real audience.

B

Convert the pattern into a testable question

For example: “Do new administrators actually cite setup time as a barrier?” This makes the next research action concrete.

C

Choose human validation matched to the decision

Interviews can test language and motives; a well-designed survey can estimate prevalence; experiments can examine outcomes. Seek specialist methods support for high-stakes work.

When not to rely on it

Do not use synthetic output as the sole basis for consequential financial, health, legal, safety, employment, or public-policy decisions. Those contexts need evidence appropriate to their stakes and affected people.

Keep the boundary close to the result.

Likerts makes the distinction visible so you can use it for what it is good at: early hypothesis generation and better preparation for human research.
