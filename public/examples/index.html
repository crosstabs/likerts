Example hypotheses

Questions to explore, not conclusions to publish.

A good Likerts prompt defines an audience and a decision. These examples show the kind of directional question a synthetic study can help sharpen before you talk to people.

These are example briefs only: they do not report actual results, customer opinions, market size, or evidence about the audiences named.

Four ways to frame a useful study

For each, the value is in the next question it helps you ask—not in treating the synthetic output as a measurement.

Product onboarding

“Among new workspace administrators at 20–200 person companies, which setup concerns might make a shared workflow product feel difficult to trial?”

Validate next: Interview recent administrators about their actual setup steps, time constraints, and decision criteria.

Positioning

“For independent retail owners considering inventory software, what value claims might feel credible and which may invite skepticism?”

Validate next: Test wording in message interviews or a controlled landing-page experiment with the intended audience.

Feature trade-off

“Among frequent business travellers, what trade-offs might influence interest in a faster expense-capture workflow?”

Validate next: Observe current workflows and survey a suitable sample about the prevalence and importance of each friction.

Retention risk

“For first-time team leads using a scheduling tool, which early experiences might contribute to abandonment after a trial?”

Validate next: Pair customer interviews with product analytics to distinguish plausible explanations from actual behaviour.

Before you run a study

Include

  • A concrete audience and context.
  • A decision, behaviour, tension, or trade-off.
  • Assumptions you are willing to examine.
  • Relevant public source context, when available.

Avoid

  • Asking the model to certify a fact or predict a market number.
  • Presenting synthetic counts as sample sizes.
  • Using a generated verbatim as a customer quote.
  • Skipping human validation for a material decision.
